# Aircraft energy models

Model identity: `aircraft-electrical`, version `1`. Presets are synthetic, editable demonstrations with `calibration: illustrative`; no measured aircraft dataset is bundled. A user-supplied measured profile retains its provenance in mission JSON. Missing battery or power model prevents energy feasibility while available RF budgets remain inspectable.

## Fixed-wing kernel

The drag polar follows NASA's [drag equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/drag-equation/) and [induced-drag coefficient](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/induced-drag-coefficient/): dynamic pressure q = ρV²/2, CL = mg/(qS cos φ), drag D = qS(CD0 + k CL²) + qAadditional. The configured induced-drag factor represents k; additional antenna drag area means equivalent coefficient times area (CdA). Only airborne equipment adds mass and drag to aircraft propulsion.

Electrical propulsion power is DV/η + mg max(vertical speed, 0)/η. Efficiency is the effective conversion from aircraft electrical input to useful propulsive power. No descent regeneration is credited. The current quasi-steady approximation retains level-load lift in climbs, conservatively adding climb work; it does not model stall, roll transients, or takeoff dynamics. Those require additional measured profile data. Takeoff/landing energy is a separate allowance.

Coordinated level turning uses R = V²/(g tan |φ|), derived from the force components described in NASA's [turn explanation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/aircraft-motion-turns/). Zero bank gives a straight path with infinite radius. A required radius implies φ = atan(V²/(gR)); it must not exceed configured maximum bank. Bank increases induced drag through load factor 1/cos φ. Turn radius and bank feed the same motion/energy constraints.

Constants: g = 9.80665 m/s²; reference density ρ = 1.225 kg/m³. Density is an explicit constant model assumption rather than inferred weather or an altitude atmosphere model. The kernel also accepts a specified positive density. Configured vertical-speed limits apply symmetrically to climbs and descents until separate measured limits are supplied. Airspeed must exceed the absolute vertical component. A profile's cruise speed is not a claimed stall envelope.

Independent hand fixture (recorded before kernel execution): mass 2 kg, S = 0.5 m², CD0 = 0.03, k = 0.06, η = 0.8, V = 20 m/s, zero bank/climb. Then q = 245 Pa, weight = 19.6133 N, parasite drag = 3.675 N, induced drag = 0.18841544664 N, electrical power = 96.585386166 W. At 45° bank, R = 40.78864851911713 m and induced drag doubles to 0.37683089328 N. A 2 m/s climb adds 49.03325 W; equivalent additional CdA = 0.01 m² adds 61.25 W. `tests/fixed-wing.test.ts` requires agreement within 10⁻⁹ W/m (10⁻¹⁰ N). These are arithmetic validation tolerances, not field accuracy.

The kernels feed manual and automatic route timing, energy and reserve analysis. Complete RF evidence, user constraints and admissible motion are also required for overall feasibility; numerical power fixtures alone do not establish an operational flight envelope.

## Multirotor kernel

Cruise airspeed (m/s) and propulsive efficiency (fraction, greater than zero through one) are editable. The illustrative preset uses 10 m/s and 0.65; neither is a measured claim. Older version-1 profiles import these fields as null, preserving editability and withholding energy feasibility until supplied.

The power table contains electrical watts at reference mass versus horizontal airspeed. Piecewise linear interpolation is bounded by its highest measured speed; a missing zero-speed row is anchored by the configured hover power. An existing zero-speed row must match hover power. No high-speed extrapolation is permitted. Multiply table power by (total mass/reference mass)^mass exponent; add climb coefficient × positive vertical speed × mass ratio. Additional antenna CdA contributes ρV³CdA/(2η). Efficiency applies to this added mechanical work, not to the already electrical table. Positive acceleration work adds m max(a_forward V + a_vertical climb, 0)/η; braking and descent receive no energy credit.

The quasi-steady motion check bounds total acceleration magnitude. Required incremental tilt follows horizontal thrust from acceleration and added equipment drag divided by vertical thrust m(g + vertical acceleration). It rejects tilt above the configured maximum and nonpositive upward thrust. The table retains baseline aircraft aerodynamic/rotor effects; the incremental model does not reconstruct those forces from electrical measurements. This is a documented engineering approximation, with no roll/pitch transient or rotor motor saturation model.

Independent fixtures: 2 kg/reference 2 kg, hover 200 W, table 0 m/s = 200 W and 10 m/s = 300 W, mass exponent 1.5, climb coefficient 50 W per m/s, η = 0.5. Hover is 200 W; 2 m/s climb is 300 W; 5 m/s interpolation is 250 W. Doubling mass gives 565.685424949238 W hover. CdA = 0.01 m² at 10 m/s adds 12.25 W, for 312.25 W total. Numerical tolerance is 10⁻⁹ W. Tests also reject acceleration, tilt, climb and table-support violations.

## Time, equipment and wind integration

Each segment integrates electrical watts times seconds divided by 3600. Propulsion includes only the airborne antenna mass and equivalent CdA. Aircraft avionics and the airborne downlink transmitter's electrical input are separate energy terms. Efficiency-based radio draw is idle watts + 10^((output dBm − 30)/10)/amplifier efficiency. Idle draw covers the airborne receiver/quiescent electronics in this radio abstraction. A measured table gives total airborne radio electrical input, interpolated linearly in dBm with no extrapolation. Ground uplink transmitter power never consumes the flight battery. A user modelling separate onboard radios must include their receive/idle electronics in the supplied total idle/table values.

Takeoff/landing energy is charged once per mission. Segments use configured cruise speed, with the allowance covering start/stop transients at mission endpoints. A sharp manual waypoint or immediate turnaround lacks finite turn/acceleration geometry and is reported as a motion violation; its constant-speed energy is a diagnostic estimate, not an accepted flight. Automatic routing supplies admissible analytic curves and independently computed motion at every sample; see [route verification](ROUTE_SEARCH.md). Multirotor pitch and roll use the incremental forces required by acceleration and added antenna drag; baseline trim from the electrical table is unknown and approximated level. Fixed-wing pitch and heading use the wind-corrected air vector.

Wind direction is the bearing wind comes FROM. For a unit ground track u and wind w, progress = w·u + sqrt(V² − |w|² + (w·u)²). Negative radicand or nonpositive progress rejects the motion. Fixed-wing V is total airspeed; multirotor V is horizontal airspeed, with climb derived from route slope. Pure vertical multirotor travel uses the configured vertical-speed limit and power at horizontal wind speed; fixed-wing vertical-only travel is rejected. Return segments solve wind and air heading independently.

Energy feasibility compares total Wh against battery Wh × (1 − required reserve). The reported remaining reserve is 1 − total/battery and can be negative. A 10⁻⁹ Wh arithmetic tolerance applies at equality. Missing model, battery, cruise/efficiency or measured table support withholds the corresponding energy evidence. Evaluated impossible motion or reserve failure is infeasible.

Hand fixture: constant 240 W multirotor propulsion over 18 km at 10 m/s is 1800 s and 120 Wh. Add 5 W avionics (2.5 Wh), 20 dBm airborne RF output with η = 0.4 and 1 W idle (0.625 Wh), and 3 Wh takeoff/landing, yielding 126.125 Wh. A 1 km eastbound fixed-wing segment at 20 m/s in 5 m/s west wind takes 40 s; reverse travel takes 66.666666666667 s. A 5 m/s north crosswind takes 51.63977794943222 s with air heading 75.52248781407008°. Fixtures check these values within 10⁻⁹ and reject impossible progress and reserve exceedance.
